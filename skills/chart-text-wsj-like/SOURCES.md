# Sources: chart-text-wsj-like

Format of every entry: title and URL, `Licence:`, `Use:` (`adapted` = text
paraphrased and restructured under the licence's terms; `cited` = idea or
finding only, no text taken).

Note on naming: this skill is modelled on The Wall Street Journal's graphics
practice as reported in secondary summaries of Dona M. Wong's guide. It is
named and described as "WSJ-like", never as the
Journal's own style; it claims no affiliation or endorsement and uses none of
the newspaper's fonts, logos or text. Numeric limits from those summaries (line
count, two-thirds height, bar/gap ratio) were not verified against the book
and are presented as guidance.

- **Dona M. Wong, *The Wall Street Journal Guide to Information Graphics* (W. W. Norton, 2010)** <https://wwnorton.com/books/9780393347289>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (book)
  - Use: cited — principles as reported in the three summaries below. No book text used.
- **Ataccama, "Top 10 takeaways from the WSJ Guide to Information Graphics"** <https://www.ataccama.com/blog/top-10-takeaways-from-wsj-guide-to-information-graphics>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (article)
  - Use: cited [V, read 2026-10-15] — reported principles: readable at a glance, do the calculations for the reader; colour and decoration only when they carry information (no 3D, rotation); context through titles, annotation and historical comparison; adjust the scale or switch to % change to fit the message; sparse legible text without all-caps, angled text or acronyms; process research → edit → plot → review; footnote missing data, drop series with too many gaps, no pie with a missing slice; pictograms for small whole-unit counts; choose the right average; bars from zero about twice the gap width, about four lines (never more than five), line filling about two thirds of the height, natural increments 1-2-5-10. Paraphrased.
- **Exec-Comms, "Book Review: The Wall Street Journal Guide to Information Graphics, by Dona M. Wong" (2011)** <https://www.exec-comms.com/blog/2011/12/19/book-review-the-wall-street-journal-guide-to-information-graphics-by-dona-m-wong/>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (article)
  - Use: cited [V, read 2026-10-15] — reported: start from reliable sources, find the message, trim data, check the graphic against the sources; meaningful comparisons and a reference point; avoid over-annotation; pie ordering from 12 o'clock. Paraphrased.
- **Blinkist summary of Wong's guide** <https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/the-wall-street-journal-guide-to-information-graphics-en>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (summary)
  - Use: cited [S, seen via search excerpt; page not readable from this environment] — reported: one colour per variable, palettes of basic colours with shades; direct labels with legends only when space is tight or lines overlap; thin rules every three to five table rows; round only at the last step; zero baseline in bar charts; pie slice order. Paraphrased.
- **FlowingData, "Review: The Wall Street Journal Guide to Information Graphics" (2010)** <https://flowingdata.com/2010/02/18/review-the-wall-street-journal-guide-to-information-graphics/>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (article)
  - Use: cited — secondary description of the guide's scope. No text used.
- **UXPA magazine review of the guide** <https://oldmagazine.uxpa.org/?p=3923>
  - Licence: Copyrighted (article)
  - Use: cited — secondary description. No text used.
- **UK Government Analysis Function, "Data visualisation: charts"** <https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/>
  - Licence: OGL-UK-3.0
  - Use: adapted — headline title plus statistical subtitle; label axes and series; horizontal text only; thousands separators; direct labelling over legends with legend order matching the chart; about ten light-grey gridlines at most; about four lines per line chart (also reported for Wong, above). Paraphrased; no images used.
